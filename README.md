# A/B Testing Analysis: Conversion Rate & Business Impact

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes the results of an A/B test designed to evaluate whether **Variant B** performs better than **Variant A** in terms of user conversion and order value.

The analysis combines exploratory data analysis, data cleaning, outlier detection, KPI evaluation, statistical hypothesis testing, and business interpretation to determine whether the observed differences between the two groups are statistically significant and actionable from a business perspective.

The core business question is:

> **Does Variant B improve conversion compared with Variant A, and does this improvement translate into a statistically significant increase in order value?**

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

The main objectives of this project are to:

* Compare the performance of A and B test groups.
* Calculate and compare conversion rates.
* Analyze average order value across groups.
* Identify and remove potentially misleading outliers.
* Test whether observed differences are statistically significant.
* Translate statistical findings into actionable business recommendations.
* Prioritize potential product hypotheses using ICE and RICE frameworks.

---

The dataset contains transactional information with the following variables:

The initial transactional dataset contains **1,197 records**.

---

## 🧹 Data Preparation & Cleaning

Data quality was assessed before conducting the A/B test analysis.

### Cross-group contamination

The analysis identified **58 visitors who appeared in both A and B groups**.

These users were removed to prevent cross-group contamination and preserve the integrity of the experiment.

### User-level order outliers

The 95th percentile of orders per user was 1, while the 99th percentile was 2.

Users with more than 2 orders were therefore treated as potential anomalies for the filtered analysis.

### Revenue outliers

The 99th percentile of order revenue was **830.3**.

Several extreme transactions were identified, including orders of approximately **3,120** and **19,920**.

These observations could disproportionately influence average order value and were excluded from the filtered analysis.

## 🔬 Exploratory Data Analysis

The analysis examined:

* cumulative revenue by test group;

* orders per user;

One notable observation was that Group B accumulated substantially higher cumulative revenue during the experiment.

However, cumulative revenue alone cannot establish statistical superiority because it can be strongly affected by traffic distribution and extreme transactions.

## 📈 Key KPIs

### Conversion Rate

| Group | Conversion |
| ----- | ---------: |
| A     |      2.97% |
| B     |      3.38% |

Group B achieved approximately a **13.8% relative improvement** in conversion compared with Group A.

### Average Order Value

Raw data:

| Group | Average Order Value |
| ----- | ------------------: |
| A     |              115.90 |
| B     |              145.06 |

Although Group B has a higher observed average order value in the raw dataset, statistical testing does not confirm that this difference is significant.

---

## 🧪 Statistical Testing

### Conversion Rate Test

**Null Hypothesis (H0):**

There is no statistically significant difference in conversion between Groups A and B.

**Alternative Hypothesis (H1):**

There is a statistically significant difference in conversion between Groups A and B.

Significance level:

`α = 0.05`

Results:

* Z-statistic: **-2.27**
* p-value: **0.0232**

Since:

`p-value < 0.05`

the null hypothesis is rejected.

### Result

There is a statistically significant difference in conversion between the two groups, with **Group B performing better**.

---

## 💰 Average Order Value Test

An independent two-sample t-test was used to compare average order values.

Raw data results:

* Group A: **115.90**
* Group B: **145.06**
* p-value: **0.3745**

Since:

`p-value > 0.05`

there is insufficient statistical evidence to conclude that the average order value differs between the two groups.

---

## 🧹 Filtered Data Results

After removing identified outliers:

| Group | Average Order Value |
| ----- | ------------------: |
| A     |              103.16 |
| B     |               99.94 |

T-test:

* t-statistic: **0.378**
* p-value: **0.7054**

The difference remains statistically insignificant.

Importantly, the conversion advantage of Group B remains statistically significant after filtering, indicating that the conversion result is not driven solely by extreme transactions.

---

## 🔎 Key Findings

### 1. Group B has a statistically significant conversion advantage

Conversion increased from **2.97% in Group A to 3.38% in Group B**.

The p-value of **0.0232** indicates that this difference is statistically significant at the 5% significance level.

### 2. The conversion result is robust to outlier filtering

The conversion advantage remains after removing anomalous users and unusually large orders.

### 3. Higher raw AOV in Group B is not statistically significant

Although Group B has a higher raw average order value, the statistical test does not provide sufficient evidence that the difference is systematic.

### 4. Extreme orders can distort revenue-based metrics

Very large transactions can significantly influence mean-based KPIs such as average order value and cumulative revenue.

### 5. Conversion is the strongest validated KPI

The most reliable statistically supported improvement in this experiment is the increase in conversion.

---

## 💡 Business Insights

The experiment suggests that Variant B has a positive effect on users' likelihood of completing a purchase.

However, an improvement in conversion should not automatically be interpreted as an increase in revenue.

For a complete business decision, conversion should be evaluated together with:

* Average Order Value;
* Revenue per Visitor;
* Total Revenue;
* Customer Lifetime Value;
* Retention;
* Long-term monetization.

Based on the current experiment, the strongest evidence supports **conversion improvement**, rather than revenue improvement.

## 🚀 Business Recommendations

### 1. Consider rolling out Variant B

If conversion is the primary business KPI, Variant B should be considered for implementation because it demonstrates a statistically significant improvement.

### 2. Continue monitoring monetization KPIs

Revenue and average order value should be monitored after rollout to determine whether the conversion uplift translates into sustainable financial impact.

### 3. Investigate extreme transactions

Large transactions should be investigated to determine whether they represent legitimate customer behavior, VIP orders, special purchases, or potential data-quality issues.

### 4. Run a follow-up experiment

A follow-up A/B test should be conducted over a longer period to validate the stability of the conversion uplift and measure its financial impact.
